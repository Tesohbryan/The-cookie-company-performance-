# The-cookie-company-performance-
Performance for the cookie company according to country sales 

# Objective
To analyze global sales performance across time, geography, and product lines, identifying key revenue drivers and underlying seasonal trends to help business stakeholders optimize inventory, marketing, and distribution.
Key Insights & Findings
• Seasonal Trends:
Pikes & Peaks: Distinct sales surges occur in April (Spring/Easter season), June (Summer start), and September-November (Back-to-School & Autumn festival rush).
• Lulls: A notable post-holiday dip occurs from January to March due to reduced post-holiday consumer spending, with lighter mid-year drops in May and late December.

# Geographic Distribution: The United
Kingdom represents the top-performing market, closely followed by India, the United States, Malaysia, and the Philippines.
Product Performance: Classic, rich flavours lead consumer demand.
1. Chocolate Chip (Top Seller)
2. White Chocolate Macadamia
3. Oatmeal Raisin
4. Snickerdoodle
5. Sugar
6. Fortune Cookie

# Strategic Business Impact
Inventory Optimization: Scale up stock and supply chain readiness ahead of the April and September-November demand surges.
Targeted Marketing: Launch promotional campaigns and health-focused messaging during slow periods (January-March) to smooth out revenue drops.
• Regional Tailoring: Focus expansion and promotional spending in top markets (UK & India) aligned with localised holidays like Easter and Diwali.
# Geographic & Product Insights
• UK as Top Market: Because the UK is your highest-performing market, its calendar heavily dictates your overall sales curve (e.g., Easter, UK school holiday schedules, and traditional tea-time snack culture).
• India & US (2nd & 3rd): In India, major festival seasons (like Diwali, which falls around October/November) strongly align with your Q3/Q4 sales surge. In the US, Thanksgiving (November) is a huge driver.
• Chocolate Chip Dominance: Classic flavours dominate sales volume. Lower-performing cookies like Fortune Cookies or Sugar Cookies are likely treated as novelty items or niche seasonal purchases rather than everyday staples.

# Potential Drivers for Sales Fluctuations
January - March (The Post-Holiday Dip):
Sales drop sharply after the festive season.
Consumers are typically cutting back on discretionary spending after holiday expenses and focusing on health-conscious New Year's resolutions (e.g., cutting down on sugar).
• April Spike (Spring/Easter Peak): Easter weekend and spring holidays typically bring a strong uptick in bakery and confectionery sales, driven by gifting, family gatherings, and holiday catering.
• May Dip vs. June Spike (Early Summer Reset
& School's Out): May often experiences a quiet lull between Easter and summer. The June surge aligns with the start of summer breaks, school graduation events, and increased tourism/outdoor activities.
• Mid-July - August Slight Drop (Mid-Summer Shift): While better than January, mid-summer can see a mild dip as families go away on vacation or opt for cold desserts (e.g., ice cream) over baked cookies.
• September - November Surge (Back-to-School & Autumn Season): September marks back-to-school routines and corporate offices resuming normal operations. October and November build major momentum with Halloween, Bonfire Night (UK), and early preparation for the holiday season.
Late November - December Drop: While counterintuitive for a bakery, a late Q4 drop often happens if your data tracks
wholesale/B2B shipments rather than direct retail sales-retailers stock up heavily in Oct/Nov, leading to lower reorders in December. Alternatively, it can indicate stockouts or supply chain bottlenecks during peak holiday demand.
